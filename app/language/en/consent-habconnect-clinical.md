# Additional consent — HabConnect, clinical study arm

> The German text is the binding version of this document. This is a
> translation provided for convenience.

This consent applies **in addition to** the general HabConnect consent
document. It does not replace it, but it **changes some of its statements** —
which ones is set out under "What changes compared with the general consent".

You are receiving this document because you were invited to take part through
⟦name of the cooperating clinical institution⟧, and this study arm requires
identity verification.

**Controller:** Digital Health research group, Faculty of Business and
Economics, Technische Universität Dresden.
**Ethics approval:** ⟦reference and date of the ethics approval⟧.

---

## The essentials

- **What is different?** In this study arm the study team knows which person is
  taking part. In the publicly available version of the app it does not.
- **Why?** So that we can reach you if a health problem comes to light during
  the study, and because a clinical study must be open to regulatory
  inspection.
- **Who sees my name?** Only the site study staff and the study management.
  **Not** the researchers who analyse your data.
- **Where is my name held?** In a separate, encrypted identity register — not
  in the research database.
- **Can I withdraw?** At any time, without giving reasons.

---

## What changes compared with the general consent

The general consent document states that all data is collected **fully
anonymised** and that neither name nor date of birth is stored. **That does not
hold for this study arm.** Instead:

| General consent                            | In this study arm                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| No name and no date of birth are stored    | Name, date of birth and contact details are stored — separately from your study data        |
| You cannot be identified from the data     | You can be identified, but only under the conditions below and only on the record           |
| Specific data cannot be deleted on request | Your register entry can be deleted specifically; your study data is retained pseudonymously |

Everything else in the general consent — that participation is voluntary, how
the study runs, use of the data for research and for the open habit database,
and international sharing of **anonymised and aggregated data only** — applies
unchanged.

## What is collected, and where it is held

| Data                                                      | Where it is stored                          |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Name, date of birth, contact details, site assignment     | **A separate, encrypted identity register** |
| Your study data (habits, recommendations, questionnaires) | The platform's research database            |

Both systems run on servers at Technische Universität Dresden, but they run
separately and use separate databases.

The research database does **not** contain your name. It knows only a study
code such as `TUD-ICU01-0042`. You can see that code yourself in the app's
settings; quote it when you contact the study team.

**No data is taken from your medical records.** Everything that enters the
study is entered by you, in the app. The clinical institution has no access to
your study data.

### How the register is protected

- Every individual field is stored encrypted (AES-256-GCM). A copy of the
  database on its own cannot be read without the key.
- The key is not held in the database; it is kept separately from it.
- Read access to the register is granted individually — belonging to the study
  team is not sufficient on its own.
- There is **no function that discloses several people at once.** Technically,
  identification is only ever possible for one person at a time.

## Who can identify you, and under what conditions

Researchers analysing your study data **cannot** determine who you are. This is
not merely a promise on paper: anyone with access to the research data is not
granted access to the register by the system.

Identification is possible only when **all** of the following hold:

1. there is a documented reason — a serious adverse event, a safety report, a
   regulatory inspection, your own request, or the correction of a data error,
2. a **second person** approves; whoever raises the request may not approve it
   themselves,
3. the disclosure is **time-limited** (normally 60 minutes) and restricted to
   the fields expressly named and to you alone.

**Every identification is permanently recorded** — with the time, the reason,
the person acting and the fields retrieved. That record can be audited
afterwards and cannot be altered, including by the study management.

## Who at the institution sees what

| Role                          | Sees your name                  | Sees your study data |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| Site study staff              | yes                             | no                   |
| Study management / monitoring | only within an approved request | no                   |
| Analysing researchers         | no                              | yes (pseudonymous)   |

## How long your data is kept

Your entry in the identity register is deleted after the study ends and the
retention period set out in the ethics approval has elapsed
(⟦retention period per the ethics approval⟧). After that, your study data can
no longer be attributed to you, permanently.

Your pseudonymous study data is retained indefinitely for long-term scientific
research, as described in the general consent document.

## Withdrawal and erasure

You may withdraw your consent at any time, without giving reasons. No
disadvantage arises from withdrawing, in particular none for your medical
treatment or aftercare.

So that you know what withdrawal does:

- Your entry in the **identity register is deleted.** After that nobody can
  attribute your study data to you — the study team included.
- Study data already collected is **retained pseudonymously** and continues to
  be analysed. This is deliberate: results already published or already part of
  an analysis must remain traceable.
- This deletion **cannot be undone.** If you later wish to take part again,
  that is only possible as a new participation.

If you also want your pseudonymous study data deleted, please contact the study
team directly. While your register entry still exists that data can be found
and deleted specifically; afterwards it cannot.

## Your rights

You have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing
and data portability, and the right to object to this processing. While you are
held in the identity register we can actually exercise those rights over your
study data — that is the difference from fully anonymous participation.

**Legal basis:** your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) and Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR).
